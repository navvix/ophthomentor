Research date: 2026-10-03. All prices were observed on that date unless a different "seen" date is stated. "Official" = read directly from the vendor's page. "Secondary" = aggregator, review blog or press. iatroX (iatrox.com) is a UK/US adaptive-qbank competitor whose blog is heavily cited below for UK pricing; treat its numbers as secondary and its editorial judgements as biased. ## Key Question 1: Tier structure, prices, billing period and what separates tiers, product by product

### Takeaway
Market leaders in the US (UWorld, TrueLearn, BoardVitals, Rosh) sell **time-boxed access to one exam's bank** with 1–2 feature tiers; AMBOSS is the main exception with a **membership + metered Qbank add-on** (50 questions/month in the base plan; unlimited as a paid bundle). The UK market is dominated by **very cheap, time-boxed, per-exam packages** (Passmedicine £15–£30) with a growing number of genuinely free tiers (Passmedicine preclinical, Pastest "Knowledge", Zero To Finals, BMJ via BMA/trusts). Germany runs on **campus licences** (AMBOSS free at ~90% of universities) with a metered 500-questions/month cap and an "unlimited" Kreuz-Upgrade.

### Inferences
- Among US leaders the "tier" axis is **duration** (30/90/180/360) and the feature axis is thin (self-assessments, reset, videos). Only AMBOSS, Lecturio, Pastest (students) and Medbullets run a true good/better/best **feature** ladder, and in every one of those the top rung's defining attribute is "unlimited questions" or "the Qbank at all".
- The UK shows that a bank can be profitable at £15–£30 per exam package if question volume is very large and the brand is the default; the US shows that $350–$680 holds if the product is perceived as the exam-realism "gold standard".

## Key Question 2: Time-based vs subscription vs lifetime vs per-exam — which dominates and why

### Takeaway
Fixed-term, per-exam access (30/90/180/365 days) dominates among market leaders in both the US (UWorld, TrueLearn, BoardVitals, Rosh, Kaplan) and UK (Passmedicine, Pastest, BMJ). Rolling monthly subscriptions are used by platform/library products (AMBOSS, Lecturio, Quesmed, Osmosis). Lifetime/multi-year is rare and used as a loyalty/pre-commitment device (AMBOSS Student Life $1,199; Pastest 5-year MRCP £459.99; Quesmed 6-year £249.99; Med-Challenger lifetime $1,299).

### Cited Findings
- UWorld: 30/90/180/360/730-day terms, with longer terms bundling self-assessments and a reset — [UWorld Step 1](https://medical.uworld.com/usmle/usmle-step-1/)
- TrueLearn: 30/90/180/365 days, price per day falls with length — [TrueLearn PANCE](https://truelearn.com/product-family/physician-assistant/?item=pance)
- BoardVitals: 1/3/6 months only — [BoardVitals help](https://info.boardvitals.com/knowledge/purchasing-subscriptions-and-packages)
- Rosh: 30 days / 90 days / 1 year — [Rosh IM pricing](https://www.roshreview.com/?p=679)
- Med-Challenger (FM): monthly $35, annual $359, **lifetime $1,299** — [BoardVitals FM comparison](https://www.boardvitals.com/blog/comparing-family-medicine-qbanks/)
- AMBOSS: monthly or annual membership (rolling), Qbank bundle as 12-month add-on, Student Life one-off through PGY-1 — [AMBOSS US pricing](https://www.amboss.com/us/pricing); DE Kreuz-Upgrade 1/3/6/12 months — [AMBOSS DE Preise](https://www.amboss.com/de/preise-medizinstudierende)
- Lecturio: 3/12/24-month terms — [Lecturio Medical pricing](https://www.lecturio.com/medical/pricing/)
- Sketchy: 6/12/24-month All Access; monthly only for the small "Cases" SKU — [Sketchy explore medical](https://www.sketchy.com/explore/medical). Sketchy's 2020 model was "12-month subscriptions" — [TechCrunch Sketchy Series A](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/02/sketchy-medical-series-a-tcg-reach-capital)
- Passmedicine: 4/6/9/12-month packages, "package prices rather than monthly subscriptions" — [Passmedicine student](https://www.passmedicine.com/student); [iatroX Passmedicine free vs paid](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/is-passmedicine-free-years-1-to-3-vs-the-paid-ukmla-question-bank)
- Pastest: 5-year MRCP membership — [Pastest MRCP membership](https://www.pastest.com/products/mrcp-membership); Quesmed 6-year undergraduate bundle — [iatroX Quesmed bundle](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/quesmeds-six-year-undergraduate-bundle-better-value-than-buying-each-year)
- UWorld institutional contracts "begin at 180 days, with 360 days being the recommended minimum… extend your term into a 2-3 year contract" — [UWorld educators](https://medical.uworld.com/us/educators/)
- Spanish MIR academies sell a course cycle (one exam attempt) at €1,300–€1,880 — [Educaweb CTO MIR Lab](https://www.educaweb.com/curso/mir-lab-on-line-379002/)

### Inferences
- Per-exam time-boxing matches the buyer's job-to-be-done (pass one exam on one date), prevents "buy one month, finish the bank, cancel" leakage that a cheap rolling subscription would allow, and lets vendors price the 30-day SKU high as an anchor (UWorld's 30-day costs 60% of its 360-day).
- Rolling monthly makes sense only where the product is a reference library used daily (AMBOSS, Quesmed notes) rather than a bank you exhaust.
- Multi-year/lifetime SKUs appear where the vendor wants to lock in before the student discovers alternatives (AMBOSS Student Life at matriculation; Quesmed 6-year at year 1).

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes the SKU mix (what share of buyers pick 30 vs 180 vs 360 days).

## Key Question 3: Genuinely free tiers vs trials — limits, additions and removals

### Takeaway
Genuinely free tiers exist mainly at the **low-price/high-volume end and in Europe** (Passmedicine preclinical, Pastest Knowledge, Medbullets, Zero To Finals, Osmosis free account, Lecturio Free, Picmonic daily cap, AnkiHub free) and are usually **volume-capped** (questions per month/day or a fixed sample) rather than feature-stripped. The premium US leaders use **no free tier**: UWorld offers only a demo, AMBOSS a 5-day trial, BoardVitals 10 days, TrueLearn 5 days, Kaplan/Lecturio 7 days.

### Cited Findings
- **No free tier / demo only**: UWorld — [UWorld Step 1](https://medical.uworld.com/usmle/usmle-step-1/)

- **Stage-gated free**: Passmedicine gives the whole preclinical bank (4,700+ Qs) free for 12 months, then charges £15–£30 for the finals bank — [Passmedicine preclinical](https://passmedicine.com/preclinical/index.php); [Passmedicine student](https://www.passmedicine.com/student)
- **Content-free / feature-paid**: Medbullets gives 1,000+ questions with analytics free; pays for adaptive learning, diagnostics, notes — [Medbullets price chart](https://step1.medbullets.com/anatomy/107078/step-1-product-and-price-chart). Lecturio Free = limited videos and recall quizzes; Qbank only in Premium — [Lecturio Medical pricing](https://www.lecturio.com/medical/pricing/). Osmosis free = editors + YouTube-public videos ad-free; questions paid — [Elsevier support](https://www.elsevier.support/osmosis/answer/tools-and-resources-included-with-a-paid-osmosis-subscription)

- **Removed/added free tiers**: Pastest "Knowledge+" shows strike-through launch prices (£4.99→£9.99, £9.99→£19.99), i.e., a recent repricing upward with the £0 tier retained — [Pastest med students](https://www.pastest.com/med-students). I found no official statement from any vendor explaining why a free tier was removed or added.

### Inferences
- The pattern is consistent: products whose moat is "closest to the real exam" (UWorld) refuse free access and rely on reputation; products fighting for attention (Pastest vs Passmedicine, Medbullets vs UWorld, Lecturio vs AMBOSS) use free tiers as acquisition.
- The two most common free-tier limiters are (a) a monthly question meter (AMBOSS 50/500) and (b) a fixed sample (Pastest 100 Qs, Medbullets 1,000 Qs). Stripping explanations or analytics is not what the leaders do; Medbullets even gives analytics free.
- Passmedicine's stage-gating (free preclinical, paid finals) is a time-shifted trial: it captures users 2–3 years before the paying event.

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes free-to-paid conversion rates; no vendor statement on the rationale for removing/adding free tiers was found.

## Key Question 7: Are three-tier (good/better/best) models common? What do "unlimited" tops add?

### Takeaway
Three-tier feature ladders are the minority among qbanks: UWorld, TrueLearn, BoardVitals and Kaplan sell one SKU per exam with duration steps; Rosh uses two tiers (Basic/Premium) and only three where bundling shelf banks or adding a course; AMBOSS, Lecturio, Pastest (students) and Medbullets run Free/Base/Unlimited-style ladders. Where an "unlimited" top exists it means **unlimited questions** (AMBOSS), **the Qbank at all** (Lecturio Premium), **extra banks/courses/CME** (Rosh Premium/Standard) or **adaptive engine + diagnostics** (Medbullets PEAK).

### Cited Findings
- One SKU, duration-tiered: UWorld — [UWorld Step 1](https://medical.uworld.com/usmle/usmle-step-1/); TrueLearn — [TrueLearn PANCE](https://truelearn.com/product-family/physician-assistant/?item=pance); BoardVitals 1/3/6 months (NCLEX names them Cram/Prepare/Master) — [TestPrepInsight](https://testprepinsight.com/reviews/boardvitals-nclex-review/)
- UWorld's only feature tier: Standard vs Plus (+ Medical Videos, +$99) — [UWorld Step 1](https://medical.uworld.com/usmle/usmle-step-1/)
- Two tiers: Rosh Basic vs Premium (Premium = + 500-question ECG bank "valued at $199") — [Rosh IM pricing](https://www.roshreview.com/?p=679); three tiers only for FMCLA and Critical Care (Basic/Standard/Premium) — [Rosh FMCLA](https://www.roshreview.com/fm/longitudinal-assessment-fmcla/pricing/)
- Three-tier feature ladders: AMBOSS (Membership 50 Q/mo → + Qbank bundle unlimited → Student Life all-platform multi-year) — [AMBOSS US pricing](https://www.amboss.com/us/pricing); Lecturio (Free → Basic no Qbank → Premium Qbank + AI tutor) — [Lecturio Medical pricing](https://www.lecturio.com/medical/pricing/); Pastest students (Knowledge £0, 100 Qs → Knowledge+ unlimited 6,000+ Qs → Knowledge + Lessons) — [Pastest med students](https://www.pastest.com/med-students); Medbullets (Free 1,000+ Qs → PEAK adaptive) — [Medbullets price chart](https://step1.medbullets.com/anatomy/107078/step-1-product-and-price-chart)
- Rosh shelf bundles: Basic one specialty (~3,000 Qs) → Standard all shelf banks (~5,000 Qs) + 100 CME → Premium + Peak Performance course + mock exam — [FitGap Rosh](https://us.fitgap.com/products/023056/rosh-review) (secondary)
- Sketchy: two SKUs (All Access vs Cases Only), duration steps inside each — [Sketchy explore medical](https://www.sketchy.com/explore/medical)
- AMBOSS DE: metered base (500 Q/month) with "unbegrenzt viele Prüfungsfragen" Kreuz-Upgrade — [AMBOSS DE Preise](https://www.amboss.com/de/preise-medizinstudierende)

### Inferences
- The two proven differentiators for a top tier are (1) removing the question meter ("unlimited") and (2) adding a second asset class (videos, ECG bank, lessons, mock exams, CME). Analytics and explanations are table stakes at every tier in all products examined; none of the leaders withholds explanations from paying users.
- A Free / Core / Infinite ladder is closest to AMBOSS (DE) and Pastest Student in structure; both pair a metered or sampled free tier with an "unlimited questions" upgrade, and both sit in markets where a campus/institutional channel also exists.

### Gaps
- No vendor discloses tier mix or upgrade rates between tiers.
