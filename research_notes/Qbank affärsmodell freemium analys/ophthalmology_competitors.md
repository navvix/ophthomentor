# Competitive landscape of ophthalmology question banks (USA + Europe), as of 2026-10-03

All prices below were observed on 2026-10-03 unless a different date is stated. "Self-reported" = figure taken from the vendor's own marketing. Searches for Reddit were blocked (reddit.com/old.reddit.com not fetchable from this environment; `site:reddit.com` queries returned no Reddit hits), so trainee sentiment relies on Student Doctor Network (SDN), Trustpilot, Eye News (UK trainee magazine), and vendor-hosted testimonials.

## Key question 1: Price, billing period and tier structure of each major product

### Takeaway
The market is dominated by two US paid products with no real free tier: OphthoQuestions (USD 329.99 / 6 mo, USD 359.99 / 12 mo, ~4,500 questions incl. add-ons, 29-question free trial) and the AAO's BCSC Self-Assessment Program (USD 299 member / USD 429 nonmember for 12 months, "4,500+" questions, no free trial). UK/European FRCOphth and EBO prep is served by smaller banks (Eyedocs eyeQ, eFRCOphth, Ophthobank, Eye Academy) whose prices are not publicly indexable; genuinely free products are flashcard/text resources (EyeGuru, EyeRounds Anki, OphthoBook, Cybersight EyeQ) rather than full exam-style qbanks.

### Inferences
- OphthoQuestions' effective price is flat (USD 55/month at 6 months vs USD 30/month at 12 months), so the 12-month plan is the de-facto default; a resident buying the full stack (base + Premium + FRCOphth Pt 2) pays ~USD 760/yr.
- The "4,558" homepage number vs ">3,400" base-plan number implies roughly 1,100 questions are paywalled behind add-ons; OphthoQuestions therefore already runs a de-facto multi-tier model (trial 29 Q → base 3,400 Q → base+premium add-ons).
- OphthoQuestions appears to have raised prices by roughly USD 10–15 recently (cached USD 314.99/349.99 vs live USD 329.99/359.99); the exact date is unknown.
- The AAO's BCSC SAP grew from ~1,600 questions (2018) to "4,500+" (2026), so a 7,000-question bank would be the largest marketed in the field — but marketed counts are self-reported and include varying question types.
- UK FRCOphth banks appear to be priced in GBP at a lower level than US products (one trainee calls eFRCOphth "budget-friendly"), but this is inferred from reviews, not observed prices.
- A 7,000-question "Infinite" tier priced below OphthoQuestions' USD 359.99 would undercut the only US product that openly claims to be "50% cheaper than the next best site".

## Key question 2: Free tiers, free trials and sample questions

### Takeaway
Among exam-style qbanks, only OphthoQuestions publishes a free tier (29 questions, feature-limited, no time limit stated) plus an ungated demo; eFRCOphth offers a "free demo"; the AAO BCSC SAP offers nothing free. Fully free offerings in the niche are flashcards, textbooks and small quiz sets (EyeGuru, EyeRounds Anki, OphthoBook, Cybersight EyeQ 200 MCQs, Eyedle 6/day), not board-style banks.

- A free tier of several hundred full-explanation board-style questions would be an order of magnitude larger than any free exam-style offer currently on the market (29 at OphthoQuestions; 200 at Cybersight, which are not exam-calibrated).
- Because existing free products are flashcard/text based, trainees already pair free Anki (EyeRounds) with one paid qbank; a free qbank tier therefore competes for the "second resource" slot rather than displacing the paid incumbents outright.

### Gaps
- Whether Eyedocs, Ophthobank or Eye Academy offer free sample questions (pages unreadable).
- Whether the OphthoQuestions 29-question trial expires (no time limit stated anywhere found).

## Key question 4: Institutional / residency-program licensing

### Takeaway
Both US incumbents sell to programs: the AAO publishes bulk discounts (20% off for 2–15 users, 30% off for 16+), and OphthoQuestions offers unpublished "group rates for groups of 10 or more" with program-director result access when the program pays.

### Cited Findings

## Key question 7: Evidence that a "free" model works or fails in this niche

### Takeaway
No ophthalmology qbank was found that went free, removed a free tier, or shut down; the only hard evidence is that (a) EyeQuiz.net's domain no longer resolves, (b) free offerings persist only when subsidized (university: EyeRounds; NGO: Cybersight/Orbis; pharma: Théa Prep'EBO; volunteer residents: EyeGuru), and (c) the paid incumbents have grown counts and prices, not cut them.

### Cited Findings
- eyequiz.net and www.eyequiz.net return DNS ENOTFOUND (2026-10-03) and no search result references a product by that name — [eyequiz.net](https://eyequiz.net/)
- EyeRounds Anki is university-hosted and free, last updated 2024-06-18 — [EyeRounds](https://eyerounds.org/anki_cards/anki_home.html)
- Cybersight is operated by Project Orbis International (NGO), free account — [Cybersight](https://cybersight.org/)
- Prep'EBO is run by Théa Academy (pharma-sponsored education arm) — [Théa Academy](https://www.thea-academy.com/education-program/prep-ebo/)
- EyeGuru has remained free for a decade (2016–2026) on a volunteer resident model — [Ophthalmology Times 2016](https://www.ophthalmologytimes.com/view/eyeguruorg-seeks-deliver-exactly-what-residents-need); [Iatrox 2026](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/eyeguru-review-short-ophthalmology-practice-before-your-first-placement)
- OphthoQuestions raised list prices (cached USD 314.99/349.99 → live USD 329.99/359.99) and keeps a 29-question trial rather than a free tier — [pricing](https://ophthoquestions.com/help/pricing)
- AAO BCSC SAP grew from 1,600+ to 4,500+ questions while priced at USD 299/429 with no trial — [AAO Store](https://store.aao.org/basic-and-clinical-science-course-self-assessment-program.html)
- OphthoCards (2025) launched directly as freemium (free flashcards + USD 7.99/mo) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6743734600)
- EBO explicitly disclaims all commercial qbanks — [EBO](https://www.ebo-online.org/ebo-exam/curriculum-recommended-reading/)

### Inferences
- Free exam-style ophthalmology qbanks have historically existed only with an external subsidy; there is no evidence of a self-sustaining free-to-paid conversion model in this niche yet, so a Free/Core/Infinite entrant would be the first to test it at scale.
- The incumbents' lack of a meaningful free tier, combined with recurring complaints about dated content and apps, is the main structural opening; the main risk is that programs pay for the incumbent regardless of resident preference.

### Gaps
- No documented shutdown or pricing-model reversal of any ophthalmology qbank was found; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence (Wayback Machine could not be fetched from this environment).
- Conversion rates or revenue of freemium medical apps in ophthalmology: none found.
